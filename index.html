Willkommen zur Wiesn

What is Oktoberfest?

Oktoberfest is the world's largest folk festival — a 16-to-18-day explosion of beer, brass bands and Bavarian tradition held every year in Munich. It started in 1810 as the wedding party of Crown Prince Ludwig and Princess Therese, and the festival grounds still carry her name: the Theresienwiese (Therese's meadow), which everyone just calls die Wiesn.

What it's actually like today

Today the Wiesn is two festivals in one. By day it's wholesome and family-friendly: fairground rides, roast chicken and pretzels, oompah bands and grandparents in their finest Tracht. By night the giant tents turn into roaring singalongs where thousands stand on the benches, arm in arm, belting out the same songs. It's equal parts centuries-old tradition and the biggest international party on earth — people fly in from everywhere, but it's still unmistakably, proudly Bavarian.

It kicks off at noon on the opening Saturday when the Mayor of Munich taps the first keg and shouts “O'zapft is!” (“It's tapped!”). Only six Munich breweries are allowed to pour — Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten — and their special Oktoberfestbier (~6% ABV) is served only in the one-litre Maß. Which is exactly as much beer as it sounds like.

    👗 What to wear — Tracht

    Half the fun is the outfit. Women wear a Dirndl (bodice, blouse, skirt + apron), men wear Lederhosen (leather shorts with suspenders). You don't have to — but almost everyone does, and you'll feel left out in jeans.

    🎀 The apron-bow secret (nobody tells tourists this): the side a woman ties her Dirndl bow means something — left = single, right = taken/married, centre-front = virgin or undecided, back = widowed (or she's a waitress). Tie — and read — carefully.

    🚇 Getting around — ÖPNV

    Munich's network is the MVV: U-Bahn, S-Bahn, tram & bus, all on one ticket.

    • Travelling as a group? The Gruppentageskarte (group day ticket, up to 5 people) is by far the cheapest way to get around together all day.
    • Here a while? The Deutschland-Ticket (~€58/month) covers all local & regional transport across Germany.
    • Airport trips reach the outer fare zones — buy a ticket covering zones M–5.
    • Get the MVGO or MVV app to buy tickets on your phone; DB Navigator for trains.
    • Validate paper tickets before boarding — ticket checks are frequent and fines are steep.

    ⛺ Tent rules & etiquette

    • You need a seat to be served — no seat, no beer.
    • Reservations are usually needed evenings & weekends; weekday mornings you can often walk in.
    • Standing on the benches = encouraged. Standing on the tables = instantly thrown out.
    • Bag rule: only small bags up to 3 litres are allowed on the grounds — leave the backpack at the flat.
    • Bring cash: a Maß is ~€15+, and a €1–2 tip per round keeps the service coming.

    🍺 Beer & toasting

    • Beer comes by the Maß (1 litre, ~6% ABV). Pace yourself — it sneaks up.
    • Toast with “Prost!”, clink the base of the mugs, and make eye contact — skipping it is said to be bad luck.
    • Not into beer? Ask for a Radler (beer + lemonade) or a Spezi (cola + orange).
    • Water and non-alcoholic drinks are available too — alternate them in.

    🧭 Survival tips

    • Agree a meeting point (our Wiesn entrance) — phones die and signal jams in the crowds.
    • Watch your phone & wallet: pickpockets love the chaos.
    • Dress in layers — tents are saunas, the walk home is freezing.
    • Tents open ~09:00 (10:00 weekends); on weekends arrive early before they close the doors when full.
    • Last call is ~22:30 — then everyone pours out toward Theresienwiese U-Bahn and Hackerbrücke S-Bahn (but we'll be gone by then).
